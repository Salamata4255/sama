Sama Privacy Policy

Effective September 15, 2026

Sama is a personal iPhone and Apple Watch app made by Salamata Bah. It compares your sleep and readiness data from your Oura ring with the meetings on your calendar, so you can see how your workday affects your night.

What Sama reads

  • Oura data. When you tap Connect, you sign in on Oura's own website and choose what to share. Sama asks only for the "Daily" scope: daily sleep and readiness summaries and the sleep periods behind them (score, bedtime, wake time, heart-rate variability). Sama does not read your email address, personal details, workouts, tags, or raw heart-rate streams.
  • Calendar data. With your permission, Sama reads events from the calendars on your device to count meetings and note when the last one ends each day. It ignores all-day events, invitations you declined, and events with no other attendees. Sama never edits your calendar and never reads event notes or attachments.

Where your data lives

Everything stays on your device. Sama has no server, no account of its own, no analytics, and no advertising. Your Oura sign-in tokens are stored in the iOS Keychain. Your sleep, readiness, and meeting summaries are stored in the app's private database on your phone, which Apple's device encryption protects. Widgets and the watch app read copies of the same on-device data.

The only network requests Sama makes are to Oura (api.ouraring.com and cloud.ouraring.com) to sign you in and download your data. Those requests are covered by Oura's privacy policy.

What Sama shares

Nothing. Sama does not sell, share, or transmit your data to anyone, including its developer.

Deleting your data

Tap Disconnect on the Connect screen to sign out of Oura and delete all downloaded Oura data and computed insights from your device. Deleting the app removes everything it stored. To revoke Sama's access on Oura's side, open the Oura app or cloud.ouraring.com and remove Sama from your connected apps.

Children

Sama is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them.
